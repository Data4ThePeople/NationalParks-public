# Availability: park budget and staff data in congressional records

Checked 2026-10-22. Facts only. We quote what each record says. We do not
guess why any data is missing. Raw copies are in `data/raw/congress/`.

## Summary

- **No park-level budget or FTE figures for FY 2024, FY 2025 or FY 2026** in
  any record we read: the FY 2026 joint explanatory statement, the House and
  Senate FY 2026 committee reports, the House FY 2027 committee report, five
  hearing records, and three GAO reports.
- **The committees asked for park-level staff data.** The FY 2026 joint
  explanatory statement tells NPS and the Department to brief the
  appropriations committees every 60 days on "staffing levels by park and
  visitor data by park." These are briefings, not published reports. We found
  no public copy of any briefing.
- **The Senate committee said in July 2025 that it had not received the FY 2026
  NPS budget justification.**
- **The only park-level staff numbers we found** are statements by members of
  Congress at hearings about a few named parks (Crater Lake, Mount Rainier,
  Olympic, Acadia). They are not official counts.

## 1. FY 2026 joint explanatory statement, Division C (Interior-Environment)

- **Law.** P.L. 119-74. Section 4 of the act says the explanatory statement
  "printed in the House section of the Congressional Record on or about
  January 7, 2026" has the same effect as a conference report statement.
- **Where printed.** Congressional Record, vol. 172, no. 5, book III, January 8,
  2026. Division C starts at H471. NPS section at H474 to H475.
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt3-PgH255.htm
- **Committee copy.** A scanned copy on the Senate Appropriations site has the
  same text. NPS section at printed pages 19 to 22.
  https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/fy26_int_jes.pdf
- **Park-level figures.** None. The NPS section gives account and program
  totals only (for example, "$2,877,195,000 for Operation of the National Park
  System"). The one named unit with a dollar figure is the Roosevelt Campobello
  International Park ("$1,956,355"), which is a commission, not a park base
  amount. The account table at the end of the division compares FY 2025
  enacted, the FY 2026 request and the FY 2026 bill by account.
- **Staff briefings by park (H474, Senate copy page 20):**

  > "NPS Staffing.--In lieu of the directive under the heading ``NPS
  > Staffing'' in Senate Report 119-46, the Committees provide only the
  > following direction: Within 60 days of enactment of this Act, and every 60
  > days thereafter, the Department and the Service are directed to brief the
  > Committees on park operations, to include staffing levels by park and
  > visitor data by park."

- **Missing construction list (H475):**

  > "At the time of this writing, the Committees have not received a list of
  > requested line-item construction projects for fiscal year 2026. Therefore,
  > the requested funding for Line-Item Construction in fiscal year 2026 has
  > been shifted in the bill to repair and rehabilitation projects within the
  > Operation of the National Park System."

- **Budget justification.** The NPS section does not say the FY 2026
  justification was not submitted. It does not mention the park table.
- **Reprogramming rule tied to staffing (H472):** "A reprogramming shall also
  consist of any significant departure from the program described in the
  agency's budget justifications. This includes all proposed reorganizations
  or other workforce actions detailed below which affect a total of 10 staff
  members or 10 percent of the staffing of an affected program or office,
  whichever is less, even without a change in funding."
- **Staffing directive in the law itself.** The act text (not the explanatory
  statement), NPS administrative provisions, Congressional Record H178:

  > "The National Park Service shall maintain staffing levels in order to
  > fulfill the mission required under title 16, title 43, and title 54, United
  > States Code, including to protect natural and cultural resources, provide
  > and maintain appropriate access and recreation for visitors, provide safety
  > precautions for visitors and staff, maintain physical and natural
  > infrastructure, provide information and respond to stakeholders and the
  > general public, conduct tribal consultation, provide for administrative
  > support, administer historic and other preservation programs, and carry out
  > other activities in support of effectively operating the National Park
  > System and carrying out programs administered by the National Park Service
  > in a timely manner."

  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-01-08/html/CREC-2026-01-08-pt1-PgH151.htm
  The same kind of sentence appears for BLM, FWS, EPA and the Forest Service.
  It sets no number of staff for any park.

## 2. Senate report S. Rept. 119-46 (FY 2026), July 24, 2025

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-119srpt46/pdf/CRPT-119srpt46.pdf

- **Park-level figures.** None for funding or FTE.
- **Budget justification not received (page 47, NPS Construction):**

  > "The Committee has not received the detailed budget justification for the
  > National Park Service for fiscal year 2026, which the Committee expects
  > will follow longstanding practice to include a project and funding list
  > along with detailed project data sheets for line item construction
  > projects that will be ready for construction and recommended for fiscal
  > year 2026 funding."

- **NPS Staffing (pages 40 to 41).** This is the paragraph the joint statement
  replaced. It asked for monthly briefings:

  > "Within 60 days of enactment of this act, and monthly thereafter, the
  > Department and the Service are directed to brief the Committee on park
  > operations, to include staffing levels by park, visitor data by park, and
  > other data necessary to convince the Committee that operations and natural
  > and cultural resource protection have not been and will not be
  > deleteriously affected in an effort to make parks operate more
  > efficiently."

- **Department-wide staffing (page 9):** "The Committee expects the
  Department of the Interior and the other agencies funded in this bill to
  maintain the requisite staffing levels to ensure that operations are not
  deleteriously affected or have a negative impact on the economic output and
  support to States, localities, and tribes."
- **Park-specific staffing directives, no numbers (page 40):** Mount Rushmore
  ("The Committee is concerned about law enforcement staffing levels at Mount
  Rushmore National Memorial" and asks for a briefing on "law enforcement
  staffing needs within 90 days") and Cumberland Island ("The Committee urges
  the Service to prioritize staffing at Cumberland Island National
  Seashore").
- **Refuge parallel (page 28, FWS section):** the report asks for
  the same kind of briefing for wildlife refuges, "to include staffing levels
  by refuge, visitor data by refuge."

## 5. Hearings

Printed transcripts exist for two hearings. For the rest we used caption text
from the official video. Caption text is machine or live captioning. It has
spelling errors and no reliable speaker labels. Quotes from captions are
copied as they appear, with the time stamp.

### Senate Appropriations, Interior subcommittee, May 21, 2025 (FY 2026)

Printed transcript: S. Hrg. 119-67,
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-119shrg60267/html/CHRG-119shrg60267.htm

- **Budget justification.** Not mentioned by name. The subcommittee chair,
  Sen. Lisa Murkowski, said of the plan to move parks to states: "So should
  this concept be included in the full budget request, I would hope that we
  have a really thorough conversation with you to better understand the
  justification for the proposal."
- **Staff counts.** Secretary Doug Burgum, answering the chair: "And then we
  got a number of about 26,500 people work in the park system. And my next
  question was, well, how many of them actually physically work in a park? And
  that answer took almost eight weeks" and "The first number I got--slightly
  less than 50 percent of the permanent employees actually work in a park."
  He also said: "We did not, in many cases, even have duty stations for where
  people reported to."
- **Park-level.** Secretary Burgum: "Some of these parks, like Yellowstone,
  they have got more people working this summer than they ever had in the
  history of the park, or certainly more than they have had." No number given.
  Sen. Patty Murray, vice chair of the full committee: "The National Park
  Service has lost 18 percent of its staff. You managed to actually fire the
  only plumber at Mount Rainier National Park."
- **Requests for park-level data.** None in the printed record.

### Senate Energy and Natural Resources, June 11, 2025 (FY 2026)

Printed transcript: S. Hrg. 119-121,
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-119shrg61237/html/CHRG-119shrg61237.htm
(Not on the original list. It is the day before the House hearing and covers
the same budget.)

- **Missing justification.** Sen. Angus King, a committee member, held up the
  FY 2025 and FY 2026 NPS documents: "This is the budget justification for the
  National Park Service for the 2025 budget that's submitted to Congress so
  that we have something we can work from and work with. That's 2025. This is
  what we have been given this year for 2026." The record inserts a
  "Side-by-side comparison of the FY25 and FY26 National Park Service Budget
  Justifications" as an image. He then said: "That's not adequate to give us
  the data that we need in order to make the oversight decisions, because we
  don't know the details of what's being done." Later: "Get the data.
  Understand the details of the budget. Give us something like this, and then
  we can decide what the plan should be."
- **Secretary's reply on staff data.** Secretary Burgum: "Well, in that thick
  book that you have there, I am certain that there is one thing that is
  missing, which is how many of those people actually work in a park, because
  we spent eight weeks this spring rebuilding all the H.R. data to find out,
  of all the people you have in your chart, how many work in a park." Also:
  "My best guess is that barely half of the people of that number actually
  work in a park."
- **Request for staff positions by location.** Sen. Murkowski, who said she is
  "the Chair of the Interior Appropriations Committee": "it would be very
  helpful if you could provide us with a full list of the BLM, the U.S. Forest
  Service, and National Park Service staff positions and their locations that
  we have lost since the new administration." We found no public copy of a
  reply.

## 7. What we could not check

- **The 60-day NPS staffing briefings.** These go to the committees. We found
  no public copy, summary or count of them on appropriations.house.gov,
  appropriations.senate.gov or govinfo.gov.
- **Printed transcripts** of the House Appropriations hearings (May 20, 2025
  and April 20, 2026), the House Natural Resources hearings (June 12, 2025 and
  May 13, 2026), and the Senate hearings of April 2026. A govinfo.gov search
  for hearings that mention "Burgum" and "budget," sorted by date, showed
  printed records through June 2026 but none of these. The govinfo API then
  hit its rate limit, so we could not run further searches.
- **Official GOP-channel YouTube captions** for the House Natural Resources
  hearings would not download. We used the committee Democrats' channel
  uploads of the same hearings.
- **C-SPAN** caption pages redirected to a paywall for automated requests.
- **congress.gov** returned HTTP 403, as before. We used govinfo.gov copies of
  the same reports.
- **Answers to the record** (written follow-up questions) for the 2026
  hearings are not published yet. The printed May 21, 2025 Senate record has
  answers, and none give park-level figures.
